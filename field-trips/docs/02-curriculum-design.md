# 02 — Curriculum Design (Grades 5–8)

This is the most important document in the repo. **The math drives the build, not
the other way around.** Every scenario must map to a specific standard and a
single clear concept. (Standards below reference Common Core State Standards for
Math — adapt if your target districts use a different framework, e.g. TEKS.)

## Why financial scenarios are great math hooks

Money is the most natural real-world home for the exact skills grades 5–8 are
learning: decimals, ratios, percentages, proportions, and early linear thinking.
The institution gives the math a *reason to exist*.

## MVP scenario spec — "Make the Deposit & Get Correct Change" (Bank, Grade 5)

**Concept:** adding and subtracting decimals in a money context (5.NBT.B.7).

**Learning objective:** the student correctly computes a remaining balance and
assembles the exact change using bills and coins.

**Flow:**
1. **Arrival / setup (narrated).** "Welcome to First Community Bank! You earned
   **$50.00** mowing lawns. You want to pay back the **$12.75** you borrowed and
   deposit the rest into savings."
2. **Step 1 — Subtraction.** "How much will you have left after paying $12.75?"
   The student selects/enters **$37.25**. Wrong answer → hint: "Line up the
   decimal points and subtract the cents first."
3. **Step 2 — Make change (manipulation).** The teller hands over a $50 bill for
   a $12.75 charge. The student grabs the correct **$37.25** in bills/coins from
   a drawer and places it in the tray. Validation checks the total.
4. **Step 3 — Deposit.** The student deposits the $37.25 into the savings slot;
   an on-screen ledger updates.
5. **Debrief.** "Nice work! You just used **decimal subtraction** to handle real
   money — the same math grown-ups use every day." Show the concept name + the
   problem they solved.

**Success criteria:** completes all three steps; wrong answers give hints and
allow retries; no fail state. Target completion time: 5–8 minutes.

**Data to log (for teachers):** student/session id, each step's attempts,
hints used, total time, final success.

## Design rules for every scenario
- **One concept per field trip.** If you're touching two standards, split it.
- **Numbers should be authentic** (real-ish prices), but kept clean enough to be
  solvable mentally or with scratch work at grade level.
- **Show the math, then have them do it** — model once, then let them try.
- **Always provide a non-reading path** (audio narration) since reading levels
  vary widely in grades 5–8.

## Open questions to resolve with an educator
- Which standard/grade do you most want to lead with for the pilot?
- Do partner teachers use CCSS, TEKS, or another framework?
- Will this be a guided classroom activity (teacher present) or independent?

> Strongly recommend recruiting a **practicing 5–8 math teacher** as an advisor
> early. They'll keep the math rigorous and grade-appropriate, which is exactly
> where well-meaning ed-tech usually goes wrong.
